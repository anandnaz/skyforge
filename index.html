New to drones? Quick guide and glossary
  1. Start with a quick-start build, or pick a frame. The frame sets the largest propeller and the stack mounting.
  2. Work down the steps. Greyed-out parts don’t work with what you’ve chosen, and each one says why.
  3. When all six core parts are chosen, check thrust-to-weight and flight time, then press Test flight.
  4. Use Undo if you change your mind, Share build to send it, and Print to keep a copy.
Frame
The body and arms. Its size decides the largest propeller.
Motor (for example 2207 2750KV)
The first four digits are the motor’s stator width and height in millimetres. KV is spin speed per volt: lower KV goes with more battery cells.
Propeller (for example 5-inch tri-blade)
Size in inches and blade count. More blades give more grip; fewer blades are more efficient.
ESC
Electronic speed controller. It powers the motors, and its amp rating must cover what they draw.
Flight controller (FC)
The drone’s brain. Its mounting pattern, such as 30x30, must match the frame and ESC.
Battery (for example 4S 1500mAh 100C)
S is the number of cells, which sets voltage. mAh is capacity. The C rating is how much current it can deliver.
Thrust-to-weight
Maximum thrust divided by weight. Around 2 can barely fly; 4 to 8 suits sport flying.
Use your own parts catalog (JSON)

Paste a catalog to replace the built-in parts with real products. Each part needs id, n (name) and w (weight in g). Optional: url, stock (in, low or out), dim (battery mm), bay (frame battery bay mm), tab (motor thrust and amps points).

Compare all options in a step

Each bar covers every complete build that works with that part. Solid = lowest value, light = up to the highest, black tick = average, ★ = best average. When your other choices fix the build, it is a single value.

Skyforge is an educational planning tool. Thrust, current and flight time are estimates, so confirm specs with the manufacturer. Charge and store LiPo batteries safely (fireproof bag, never unattended), test with propellers off, fly only in safe and legal places, and follow your local drone rules.
